# Fathom

There is no map of the abyss until you make one. Swim the flooded corridors as a
small, cold light, grazing plankton and learning the shape of the rock. Eat to
widen your circle. Stop feeding and your glow dwindles, taking the world with it.
Even familiar passages disappear beyond the edge of your vision.

Send a sonar pulse around a blind corner and watch its crest split at a junction.
The terrain it touches stays in memory, but the creatures do not. An amber glimmer
might be a drifting meal — or the bulb of a Lanternjaw, wearing a jellyfish’s
skirt until your light gives you away.

The deep has other ways to find you. Gloamfins listen through walls and answer your
sonar with their own. Flarefish charge a warm spark before burning the darkness
away. Duck into ink to break a light hunter’s fix, but do not expect the cloud to
silence you. A bright corridor is safer to read and more dangerous to inhabit.

Clear every mote to descend. Each new depth shortens your sonar’s reach and lets
more hunters out of the den. Keep a route in mind, leave yourself a turn, and
learn when to shine — and when to vanish.
